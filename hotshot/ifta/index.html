Each jurisdiction's taxable gallons are its miles divided by your fleet MPG, where fleet MPG is all miles divided by all gallons bought. Net taxable gallons are the taxable gallons minus the tax-paid gallons you bought there. A positive number is tax owed to that jurisdiction; a negative number is a credit. With no MPG typed, the net gallons across all rows always add up to zero. Tax due is net taxable gallons times that jurisdiction's rate.

The math uses exact numbers and shows cents. The official return reports fleet MPG to two decimals and rounds each column to whole gallons (IFTA Procedures Manual P720), so what you file can differ by a few cents. Fleet MPG here uses gallons bought; IFTA means fuel consumed, so adjust the gallons if your tank level changed a lot between the start and end of the quarter. Surcharges, interest, late fees and license fees are not included.

Who has to file IFTA

IFTA reporting is for a "qualified motor vehicle" used to carry people or property that meets any one of these three tests (IFTA Articles of Agreement, section R245):

  • two axles and a gross vehicle weight or registered gross vehicle weight over 26,000 lb (11,797 kg); or
  • three or more axles, regardless of weight; or
  • used in combination, when the combination's gross vehicle or registered gross vehicle weight is over 26,000 lb (11,797 kg).

Recreational vehicles used only for personal pleasure, and not in any business, are excluded (R245, R249). A person based in a member jurisdiction who operates a qualified motor vehicle in two or more member jurisdictions has to be licensed under IFTA (R305). Licensing, decals and the return itself go through your base jurisdiction.

This is a summary of IFTA's published definition, not a ruling on your truck. Which weight counts (rated, registered or actual) is for your base jurisdiction to say; ask them before you rely on it. If you are leased to a carrier, who reports depends on the lease (R530): the lessor reports on a lease of 29 days or less, and on a lease of 30 days or more the two sides can choose in writing, with the carrier reporting if nothing is written down.

IFTA due dates, 2026 returns

A quarterly return is due on the last day of the month after the quarter ends. If that day is a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, the next business day is the due date (R960.100).

Q1 (Jan - Mar): Thursday, April 30, 2026Apr 30

Q2 (Apr - Jun): Friday, July 31, 2026Jul 31

Q3 (Jul - Sep): Saturday, October 31, so Monday, November 2, 2026Nov 2

Q4 (Oct - Dec): Sunday, January 31, so Monday, February 1, 2027Feb 1, 2027

A return is required every quarter, even if you drove no miles or used no taxable fuel (R930.100). Holidays that move a date, and your base jurisdiction's own filing date, are on you to check. The Excel worksheet works out these dates for any year, but only moves weekend dates, not holidays.

Common questions

Do non-CDL hotshots have to file IFTA?

It depends on weight and axles, not on whether you need a CDL. IFTA covers a truck with two axles and a gross or registered weight over 26,000 lb, a truck with three or more axles, or a combination over 26,000 lb (R245). IFTA's board has said the axle test counts the truck's axles only, not the trailer's. The federal CDL rule is separate: a combination of 26,001 lb or more, with a towed unit rated over 10,000 lb (49 CFR 383.91). The two lines sit close together but are not the same test, so a rig can meet one and not the other. Confirm with your base jurisdiction which weight figure it uses.

Where do I get the tax rate for each state?

From the IFTA rate matrix at iftach.org. Pick the quarter you are filing, then use the U.S.-dollar rate in your fuel's column (diesel is labelled "Special Diesel" there) and read the jurisdiction's footnotes. Rates change by quarter, which is why none are built in here.

Is there an IFTA calculator by state?

Yes: this one. Add a row for each state or province you drove in. Each row's tax is worked out from its own miles, gallons and rate, and the total nets credits against tax due.

Do I file if I drove no miles, or bought no fuel in a state?

A return is required for every quarter, even if no operations were conducted or no taxable fuel was used (R930.100). Fuel you bought in a state where you logged no miles generally still goes on the return as tax-paid gallons, and shows up as a credit there.

Is this tax advice?

No. It is a planning worksheet that does arithmetic on the numbers you type. Your base jurisdiction's return is what counts, and IFTA rules, rates and due dates can change. Check them with your base jurisdiction before you file or pay.
