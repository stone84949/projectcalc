Every 1% on $21,600.00/month of revenue = $216.00/month. That's the number to have in mind when you compare factor rates: a half-point difference on this volume is $108 a month.

The cost of factoring formula: fee = invoice x factor rate + flat fees, and effective rate = fee / invoice. Flat fees matter most on small invoices - a $25 fee is 1.4% of an $1,800 invoice but 5% of a $500 one.

This calculator takes all fees out of the advance. Some factors take them out of the reserve instead; then cash today is higher and the reserve lower by the same amount, and the total you receive is the same. On the load panel, enter the effective rate (3.83% here) in FACTORING so the flat fees get counted on every load.

Common questions

What is a factor rate?

The percentage of each invoice the factoring company keeps for paying you early. It's the headline number on the contract. The factor fee is that rate in dollars, plus any flat fees per invoice or per payment.

What is the cost of factoring formula?

Fee = invoice x factor rate + flat fees. Divide the fee by the invoice for the effective rate, and multiply the fee by your invoices per month for the monthly cost. The calculator above does all three.

Why is my effective rate higher than my factor rate?

Flat fees. A per-invoice fee, an ACH fee or a wire fee is the same dollars on every invoice, so it adds more to the effective rate on smaller loads. Read the fee schedule, not just the rate.

What is the reserve?

The part of the invoice not advanced up front. With a 90% advance on $1,800, $180 is held until the broker pays the factor, then released to you, minus any fees still owed.
