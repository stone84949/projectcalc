Fixed costs are spread over every mile you drive, loaded or empty, because the truck payment doesn't care which. Deadhead is the share of total miles that are empty; the minimum rate divides by the loaded share, since only loaded miles get paid.

Which number goes in the panel? Type $1.30 into the panel's MIN $/MI NET, not $1.415. The panel already subtracts fuel, tolls and factoring from each load before it compares, so its minimum is your cost per mile without fuel, plus profit: fixed + maintenance + driver pay + profit. Both are per total mile, so they line up. If you factor, quote a little above the minimum rate: the panel also takes your factoring percent off the top, and this calculator doesn't know it.

The minimum rate per loaded mile leaves out tolls and factoring, because they change load to load. The load panel counts them. Minimums round up to the cent, and the rate is worked out from the rounded panel minimum, so a load at the quoted rate and your usual deadhead clears the panel (before tolls and factoring).

Common questions

What is a good cost per mile for hotshot trucking?

The one that's true for your truck. Insurance, payments and miles vary so much between operators that someone else's average tells you little. Put your own bills in above; the result is the floor your rates have to clear.

Should I count deadhead miles in my monthly miles?

Yes. Enter all the miles the truck runs in a month. Your fixed costs get spread over every one of them, and the deadhead % then works out how much of that driving actually gets paid.

Why is the panel's minimum lower than my cost per mile?

Because the panel takes fuel off each load first, using that load's real miles and your mpg. Putting fuel into MIN $/MI NET as well would count it twice and turn good loads into PASS.

Do I pay myself as driver pay or as profit?

Either works, just don't count it twice. If you pay yourself per mile, enter it as driver pay and set profit to what the business keeps. If you take whatever's left, leave driver pay at 0 and set profit to what you need to earn.
