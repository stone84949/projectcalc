At 150 empty miles this load nets $1.18 per mile driven, under the $1.30 minimum. It carries up to 109 empty miles before it drops below. The break-even is the most total miles the gross can cover at your minimum plus fuel, minus the loaded miles, rounded down to a whole mile.

Deadhead % here is empty miles divided by total miles (loaded + empty). Some people divide by loaded miles instead, which gives a bigger number for the same trip - 150 / 420 is 35.7%.

Net here is after fuel only, per total mile - the same basis as the panel's MIN $/MI NET. Tolls and factoring are left out; run the load through the load panel for the full take-it-or-pass with those counted.

Common questions

When to deadhead?

When the load at the other end still clears your minimum with the empty miles counted. Put the deadhead to pickup in above: if the break-even is more than the miles you'd run empty, the trip works; if it's less, counter the rate or keep looking. Deadheading toward a lane you know pays can make sense too - put those miles on the first load out and run the same check.

What counts as deadhead miles?

Any miles the truck runs without paid freight: to the first pickup, between drops and the next pickup, and home empty. If nobody is paying for the mile, it's deadhead.

Why compare per total mile instead of per loaded mile?

Because the truck costs money on every mile, paid or not. Rate per loaded mile is what the broker quotes; rate per total mile is what the trip is actually worth to the truck.

Where does my minimum come from?

From your own costs. The cost per mile calculator works it out from your truck payment, insurance and other bills - it's the same number the panel uses.
