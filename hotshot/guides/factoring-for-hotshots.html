Factoring for hotshots: the real cost of fast pay

Brokers pay in 30-45 days. Fuel pumps want payment today. Factoring bridges that gap for a percentage of the invoice - and that percentage belongs in your load math, not in the fine print.

How it works

You deliver, the broker signs the rate confirmation and the bill of lading, and you sell that invoice to a factoring company. They advance most of it the same day - typically 90% or more - and the balance when the broker pays, minus their fee. For hotshots that fee commonly runs 2.5-3.5%. On a $1,539 load at 3%, the factoring company keeps $46.17. That is the entire mystery.

Recourse vs non-recourse

Recourse means if the broker never pays the factor, the factor comes back to YOU for the money. It's cheaper for exactly that reason. Non-recourse means the factor eats a true broker credit loss (not a slow-pay or a dispute - read the exclusions; most "non-recourse" contracts carve out collections problems and disputes). Non-recourse costs more per load. If you run credit-checked brokers off the big boards, recourse plus your own credit checks is usually the better buy.

The terms that matter more than the rate

A 2.5% headline with a 12-month minimum and a reserve hold can cost you more than 3% month-to-month. Before signing, find: the minimum contract length and exit fee; the reserve amount and how fast it releases; per-invoice or per-funding fees stacked on the percentage; whether they verify credit on your brokers free; and how advances arrive (ACH same-day vs next). The percentage is the visible number. These are the ones that bite.

Is it worth it?

For a new authority with thin reserves, usually yes: 3% of gross is roughly $0.09/mi on a $2.85/mi load - real money, but cheaper than a credit line you can't get yet, and cheaper than sitting home because you can't fund the next tank. As reserves build, factor selectively (the slow-paying brokers only) or drop it and bank the difference. Either way, the honest comparison is per-load: run the same load at 0% and at your rate in the panel and look at the two stamps.

Common questions

Do I have to factor every load?

Depends on the contract - some factors want your whole book, others let you pick. If you can pick, factor the slow brokers and cash-flow the fast ones; the panel shows you exactly what each choice costs per load.

Is factoring deductible?

Generally the fee is a business expense - but tax treatment is your CPA's lane, not a calculator's. Keep the fee statements; they print themselves in your factoring account.

What's a good factoring rate for a hotshot?

Commonly quoted between 2.5% and 3.5% depending on volume, credit history, and recourse terms. The right question is total cost including fees and reserves, compared against your cost of capital - not the headline percentage alone.
