How much paint for a small bathroom?

1 gallon covers two coats on the walls of a 5×8 bathroom with 8 ft ceilings, one door and one window. Add the ceiling and buy 2.

Buy 1 gallon for two coats on the walls of a 5×8 bathroom with 8 ft ceilings, one door and one window. Painting the ceiling too takes 2 gallons.

The walls are 2 × (5 + 8) × 8 = 208 sq ft. Take off one 3×7 door (21 sq ft) and one 3×5 window (15 sq ft) and 172 sq ft is left. Two coats is 344 sq ft. At 375 sq ft per gallon, the middle of Sherwin-Williams’ 350–400 range, that is 0.92 gallons, so 1 gallon covers it. The 40 sq ft ceiling adds 80 sq ft across two coats: 424 sq ft, or 1.13 gallons, so you buy 2.

The finish you pick does not change the math here: coverage comes from the can, so type the number on your can into the coverage box. A single refresh coat on the 5×8 walls is 172 sq ft, 0.46 gallons, which rounds to 1 gallon. One coat of primer on the same walls at 250 sq ft per gallon, the middle of Sherwin-Williams’ 200–300 range, is 0.69 gallons, also 1 gallon. Taller ceilings change it: at 9 ft the same walls are 396 sq ft for two coats, 1.06 gallons, so you buy 2.

FAQ

Do I subtract the tub or the vanity?

No. The calculator takes off doors and windows only. A tub surround or vanity is some wall you will not paint, but rounding up usually covers the difference.

Can I buy a quart instead of a gallon?

Not for two coats. The 5×8 walls need 0.92 gallons, and a quart is a quarter of a gallon. A single refresh coat needs 0.46 gallons, which is still more than one quart.

What if my bathroom has no window?

Set windows to 0. The 5×8 walls then come to 374 sq ft for two coats, 0.997 gallons, which still rounds to 1 gallon, with almost nothing to spare.
