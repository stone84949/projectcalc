How much paint for a 10×10 room?

Two gallons for two coats on the walls of a 10×10 room with 8 ft ceilings. One gallon for a single coat.

Buy 2 gallons for two coats on the walls of a 10×10 room with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows. A single refresh coat in the same color fits in 1 gallon.

The walls are 2 × (10 + 10) × 8 = 320 sq ft. Take off one 3×7 door (21 sq ft) and two 3×5 windows (30 sq ft) and 269 sq ft is left. Two coats is 538 sq ft. At 375 sq ft per gallon, the middle of Sherwin-Williams’ 350–400 range, that is 1.43 gallons, so you buy 2. One coat is 269 sq ft, or 0.72 gallons, so 1 gallon covers it.

Adding the ceiling puts 100 sq ft more on every coat. Two coats on walls and ceiling is 738 sq ft, which is 1.97 gallons at 375. That still rounds to 2, but only just. If your paint lists 350 sq ft per gallon, the same job is 2.11 gallons and you need 3. Check the can, then type its number into the coverage box.

Primer covers less. Sherwin-Williams lists about 200–300 sq ft per gallon, so enter 250 and one coat for a primer coat on the walls.
