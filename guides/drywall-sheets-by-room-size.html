How many sheets of drywall for a room? 10×10 to 16×20

4×8 drywall sheet counts for walls and ceiling of a 10×10 to 16×20 room with 8 ft walls, one door, two windows and 15% waste.

A 10×12 room takes 16 sheets of 4×8 drywall for the walls and ceiling, with 8 ft walls, one door, two windows and 15% waste.

The walls are 2 × (10 + 12) × 8 = 352 sq ft. Take off the 3×7 door (21) and two 3×5 windows (30), then add the 120 sq ft ceiling: 421 sq ft. Fifteen percent waste is 484.15 sq ft. A 4×8 sheet is 32 sq ft, so 15.1 sheets, rounded up to 16.

Covered area is walls plus ceiling, minus one door and two windows. Every count includes 15% waste. The calculator also gives planning counts for the 10×12 job: 512 screws (32 per sheet) and 480 ft of tape (30 ft per sheet). Those are rough planning numbers, not a fastening schedule.

The 12×12 room has its own guide. CertainTeed’s public calculator does not subtract openings and does not add waste; ours does both, so the two often land close together.

FAQ

Should I use 4×8 or 4×12 sheets?

4×12 sheets mean fewer joints to tape, and fewer sheets in most rooms. They are heavy and hard to get up a staircase, so 4×8 is the usual DIY choice.

Is 15% waste too much?

It covers cut-outs, mistakes and the strip you throw away at the top of each wall. On a simple room you may have a sheet left over, which is cheaper than a second trip.

Does the count include a closet?

No. Add a closet as its own small room and add the sheets.
