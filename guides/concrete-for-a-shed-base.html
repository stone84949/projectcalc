How much concrete for a shed base?

An 8×10 shed slab 4 inches thick takes 47 bags of 80 lb concrete with 5% waste, about one cubic yard. Table for 8×8 to 12×16.

An 8×10 shed base 4 inches thick takes 47 bags of 80 lb concrete with 5% waste. That is just under one cubic yard.

8 × 10 × (4 ÷ 12) = 26.67 cu ft. Five percent waste brings it to 28 cu ft. Quikrete’s data sheet for Concrete Mix 1101 lists about 0.60 cu ft per 80 lb bag, so 28 ÷ 0.60 = 46.7 bags, rounded up to 47.

A 6-inch slab for an 8×10 shed is 40 cu ft and 70 bags of 80 lb. 47 bags of 80 lb is 3,760 lb of dry mix, and 112 bags is almost 9,000 lb. Above about a yard, price a short-load ready-mix delivery before you mix by hand.

This is slab volume only. Gravel base, forms, rebar or mesh, and any thickened edge or footing are separate. Check local code and the shed maker’s foundation spec before you pour.

FAQ

How thick should a shed slab be?

Four inches is common for a garden shed. Heavier loads, like a workshop or a riding mower, often use more. Follow the shed maker’s foundation spec and local code.

Do I need 5% waste?

It covers spillage and an uneven base. If your forms are dead level on compacted gravel you can set waste lower in the calculator, but do not plan to run short mid-pour.

60 lb or 80 lb bags?

Same concrete, different yield: about 0.45 cu ft for 60 lb and 0.60 cu ft for 80 lb. 60 lb bags are easier to lift; you just need more of them.
