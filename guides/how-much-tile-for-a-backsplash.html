How much tile for a kitchen backsplash?

A 15 ft run of backsplash 18 inches tall is 22.5 sq ft, or 24.75 sq ft with 10% waste: 3 boxes of 10 sq ft tile.

A 15 ft backsplash 18 inches tall needs 3 boxes of 10 sq ft tile. That is 22.5 sq ft plus 10% waste, or 24.75 sq ft.

Measure the backsplash as a rectangle: run length × height. Most backsplashes run from the counter to the underside of the wall cabinets, about 18 inches, or 1.5 ft. 15 × 1.5 = 22.5 sq ft. A straight layout adds 10% for cuts: 24.75 sq ft, so 3 boxes. With 4-inch square tile that is 223 tiles.

Box counts assume 10 sq ft per box. Subway and mosaic tile are sold by the square foot or the sheet, so read the coverage on your box and type it in. The tile count in the calculator assumes square tile; for 3×6 subway tile, go by square feet and boxes instead.

Do not subtract outlets. Subtract a window or a range hood wall only if the gap is large. Each extra wall section, like the strip behind the range, is its own rectangle: run it separately and add the totals.

FAQ

How tall is a standard backsplash?

Usually about 18 inches, from the counter to the bottom of the upper cabinets. Behind a range or on an open wall it is often taller, so measure each section.

How much extra tile should I buy for a backsplash?

10% for a straight or stacked layout and 20% for diagonal or herringbone. Small jobs leave no room for a cracked tile, so keep a spare box from the same lot.

Does a herringbone subway backsplash need more?

Yes. Use the 20% herringbone setting. Every edge of a herringbone layout gets an angled cut.
