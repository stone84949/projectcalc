How much paint for a 10×12 room (bedroom, office or nursery)?

2 gallons for two coats on the walls of a 10×12 room with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows. 3 gallons with the ceiling.

Buy 2 gallons for two coats on the walls of a 10×12 room with 8 ft ceilings, one door and two windows. Painting the ceiling too takes 3 gallons.

The walls are 2 × (10 + 12) × 8 = 352 sq ft. Take off one 3×7 door (21 sq ft) and two 3×5 windows (30 sq ft) and 301 sq ft is left. Two coats is 602 sq ft. At 375 sq ft per gallon, the middle of Sherwin-Williams’ 350–400 range, that is 1.61 gallons, so you buy 2. The 120 sq ft ceiling brings two coats to 842 sq ft, which is 2.25 gallons, so you buy 3.

Ceiling height is what pushes it up: at 10 ft the walls alone cross two gallons. Three coats on the base walls is 903 sq ft, 2.41 gallons, so 3 gallons. Type your own door and window counts, ceiling height and the coverage printed on the can into the calculator.

FAQ

Does this match the bedroom guide?

Yes. The bedroom guide table lists the same 10×12 room at 2 gallons for the walls and 3 gallons with the ceiling.

Do I need primer?

New drywall or a big color change usually does. Primer covers less than topcoat: Sherwin-Williams lists about 200–300 sq ft per gallon, so enter 250 and one coat for a primer coat.

What if I leave out the door and windows?

With no door or windows the walls come to 352 sq ft, or 704 sq ft for two coats, 1.88 gallons, still 2 gallons. Set the counts to what you really have so a tight job does not run short.
