How much paint for a living room?

3 gallons for two coats on the walls of a 15×20 living room with 8 ft ceilings, two doorways and three windows. 5 gallons with the ceiling.

Buy 3 gallons for two coats on the walls of a 15×20 living room with 8 ft ceilings, two doors and three windows. Painting the ceiling too takes 5 gallons.

The walls are 2 × (15 + 20) × 8 = 560 sq ft. Two 3×7 doors (42 sq ft) and three 3×5 windows (45 sq ft) come off, leaving 473 sq ft. Two coats is 946 sq ft. At 375 sq ft per gallon, the middle of Sherwin-Williams’ 350–400 range, that is 2.52 gallons, so you buy 3.

Living rooms often have taller ceilings. At 9 ft the same 15×20 room is 1,086 sq ft for two coats, still 3 gallons. A single refresh coat on the 8 ft walls is 473 sq ft, or 1.26 gallons, so you still buy 2. Wide openings into a hallway or kitchen are not doors: add the missing wall area back by lowering the door count.

FAQ

Should I paint the ceiling with the same paint?

Many people use a separate ceiling paint. If you do, run the walls and the ceiling as two separate calculations so each product gets its own gallon count.

What if my paint covers 350 sq ft per gallon?

Type 350 into the coverage box. For the 15×20 walls that is 2.70 gallons, still 3.

Do I count an open archway as a door?

Count it as missing wall. A 3×7 door takes off 21 sq ft; a wide archway takes off more, so add a door or window to the count until the area matches.
